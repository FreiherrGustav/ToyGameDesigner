Toy Game-Maker

Game rules
These aren't placed — they just switch on or off the moment you click them. Gravity makes everything fall. Wrap makes an edge loop around to the opposite side instead of stopping you — one switch per direction. These rules, and the camera below, belong to whichever room is open, and switch automatically with the room tabs.
Camera
Fixed shows the whole room at once. Follow lets the room be wider than the screen and keeps the camera on the player. Runner does the same, but the camera moves on its own — fall behind it and that's game over.
What to place
Pick a character
The player (only one)
Custom characters
Your own named characters, each with its own drawn art and its own way of acting — grab one to place it, or hit + to make a new one.
How it acts —
Voice (optional)
Pick an object
Real things in the room — you bump into them, and some of them (not the fan, the spawner, or the barrier) can be picked up and carried around. Pushable block is yours to redraw, like a character; the rest use a built-in icon, same as Ground.
Fixtures
Built into the room — a door, a switch, a goal, a gravity well do their job from wherever you put them, so you walk right onto them instead of bumping into them. They never move and can't be carried.
Collectibles
Things a kid scores by touching, then they're gone — grab one to place it, or hit + to draw your own.
Color
For a switch or plate — none: a switch flips gravity, a plate does nothing. Pick a color instead and it turns that color on (a switch keeps it on; a plate only while something's standing on it) — any matching-colored wall over in Ground, or barrier over here in Objects, listens for it.
For a barrier, color means the same thing it means for a wall over in Ground: none and it always blocks, forever, with no way to open it. Pick a color and it slides open while that channel is on, and slides shut again the moment it turns off — a switch holds it open for good, a plate only while something is actually standing on it.
For a door, color means something different: it's how the door decides where it leads. None keeps a door's old default (links onward automatically). A color no other door is using yet opens into a brand-new room. The same color on a second door links the two directly — no new room — which is how a real loop back to an earlier room gets built. Only two doors can ever share one color.
Ground
What it looks like, and whether it blocks you — plain none/solid, or a real look like grass, brick, or crystal. Grab one to paint it, or hit + to draw your own.
Ground Effects
Two independent choices, painted together. Ground decides what it looks like and whether it blocks you. Ground Effects decides what happens when you touch it, layered on top — none + none just clears a tile back to empty.
Hazard costs one life and knocks you back — you keep going. Lethal costs every life at once, instantly, with no knockback. Diggable blocks you until you stand there holding Down long enough to dig through. Trap looks solid to everyone else, but crumbles away the instant you touch it.
Lethals
Lava and fire (both animated) and spikes — tagged lethal automatically the moment you place one: touch it and it's game over instantly, no knockback. Grab one to paint it, or hit + to draw your own (yours will be lethal too).
Double-click any painted Ground texture or Lethal to spin it 90° — just how it looks, not how it blocks or hurts.
Color
None: this wall always blocks, same as ever. Pick a color and it only blocks while nothing of that color is currently on — a matching switch or plate over in Objects is what turns it off.
Paint
Pure decoration, zero gameplay meaning. Pick one to paint, or hit + to draw a new one — it's added to this project's kit, never swapped in for an old one.
